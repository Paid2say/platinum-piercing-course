Bloodborne Pathogens

A bloodborne pathogen is any infectious microorganism carried in human blood that can cause disease when transmitted to another person. In piercing, you work with needles, blood, and open wounds every single day. This is not theoretical — it is your daily exposure risk.

Pathogen comparison chart or BBP training visual

The Big Three

HIV — Human Immunodeficiency Virus

Attacks and destroys the immune system over time. Transmitted through blood, sexual fluids, and breast milk. Not airborne. Survives outside the body only minutes to hours. No cure, but highly manageable with antiretrovirals. Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) must be started within 72 hours of exposure.

Hepatitis B (HBV)

Attacks the liver and can cause cirrhosis, liver failure, and liver cancer. Far more infectious than HIV — survives on dry surfaces up to 7 days. A vaccine exists — every working piercer must be vaccinated. In an unvaccinated person, a single needlestick carries roughly a 6–30% transmission risk.

Hepatitis C (HCV)

Also liver-targeting and the leading cause of liver transplants in the US. No vaccine. Survives on surfaces up to 3 weeks in some conditions. Transmitted primarily through blood-to-blood contact. Now curable with antivirals — but prevention is always the goal.

Standard Precautions

The Rule
Treat every client as if they are carrying a bloodborne pathogen — because you don't always know, and they may not know either.

PPE: Nitrile gloves preferred. Change between every client and if torn mid-procedure. Eye protection for any splash risk. Mask for respiratory protection of both piercer and client.

Needle safety: One needle, one client, one time. Never recap two-handed. Needles go directly into a puncture-resistant sharps container immediately after use. Seal containers at ¾ full — never overfill.

Exposure Response

Emergency — Act Immediately
PEP for HIV must begin within 72 hours. Seek medical attention the same day as the exposure.
  1. Remove gloves carefully
  2. Wash the site with soap and water for at least 15 minutes
  3. Splash to eyes or mouth — flush with clean water for 15 minutes
  4. Report to your employer immediately
  5. Seek medical attention that day — PEP within 72 hours
  6. Document everything: time, circumstances, source client info if available
  7. Follow-up testing: baseline, 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months

First Aid in the Studio

Vasovagal Syncope — Fainting (Most Common Emergency)

Caused by anxiety, pain, sight of blood, heat, hunger, or dehydration. Warning signs: pale skin, sweating, nausea, lightheadedness. Prevention: ask about last meal and water. Lay clients flat for sensitive piercings. Response: lower safely, elevate legs, cool cloth, juice if conscious. If unconscious beyond 1–2 minutes: call 911.

Allergic Reactions

Local: redness, itching at site. Anaphylaxis: spreading hives, throat tightness, difficulty breathing, BP drop — call 911 immediately. Always ask about metal allergies at consultation.

Uncontrolled Bleeding

Direct pressure with sterile gauze. Elevate. If bleeding doesn't slow in 10 minutes or is arterial = call 911.

CPR

Check scene safety, check responsiveness. Call 911 — or direct a specific person to call. 30 chest compressions to 2 rescue breaths (or compression-only). Use AED as soon as available. Continue until EMS arrives. Red Cross and American Heart Association both offer certification. Recertify every 2 years. This is not optional.

Epidermis

Outermost layer. Avascular — no blood vessels. 5 sublayers; outermost (stratum corneum) is dead, keratinized cells. Thickness varies: eyelid ~0.5mm, palm/sole up to 4mm. Breaching it starts the wound-healing cascade.

Dermis

Contains collagen, elastin, hair follicles, sweat glands, sebaceous glands. Blood vessels live here — this is where piercing bleeds. Nerves live here — this is where the client feels pain. Most surface and lobe piercings sit largely within or just below the dermis.

Hypodermis / Subcutaneous Tissue

Fat and connective tissue beneath the dermis. Deeper piercings pass through or anchor here. More fat = more tissue movement = higher migration risk in some placements.

Critical — Cartilage
Cartilage relies on the perichondrium for nutrition and immune response. Infection can lead to perichondritis, abscess, or permanent deformity. This is why cartilage infections hit harder than lobe infections.

Key Vascular Danger Zones

Tongue: Lingual artery runs lateral — piercing goes midline only. Visible lingual vein on underside must be avoided. Nostril: Facial artery angular branch — deep lateral placements risk this. Ear: Superficial temporal artery anterior to tragus; posterior auricular artery behind ear. Genitals: Highly vascularized; dorsal artery of clitoris runs midline — VCH must respect midline anatomy.

Key Nerves by Site

Ear: Vagus nerve branch stimulated during tragus piercings — can trigger vasovagal response, warn clients. Tongue: Lingual nerve runs lateral — same risk zone as lingual artery. Too lateral = risk of permanent tongue numbness. Genital: Dorsal nerve of clitoris/penis — injury can cause permanent sensory loss.

Wound Healing Phases

Phase 1 — Hemostasis (0–hours)

Vessels constrict, platelets aggregate, clot forms. The initial crust and dried lymph clients see is from this phase — it is normal. Not infection. Teach this clearly or clients panic and over-clean.

Phase 2 — Inflammatory (Days 1–5, can extend)

Vasodilation increases blood flow. White blood cells arrive to fight pathogens and clean debris. Signs: redness, warmth, swelling, some pain — this is NORMAL. Inflammation is not infection. Infection means pathogens have colonized the tissue and are winning.

Phase 3 — Proliferative / Granulation (Weeks–Months)

New collagen is laid down. New blood vessels form. Epithelial cells migrate — the fistula (piercing channel) forms here. Downsizing happens during this phase. Too early = trauma. Too late = lever arm causing migration or irritation bumps.

Phase 4 — Remodeling / Maturation (Months–Years)

Type III collagen replaced by stronger Type I. Fistula matures and strengthens. Full tensile strength can take up to 2 years. "Healed" means fully remodeled — not just "not actively angry."

Safe Placement & Red Flags

Assess before marking: tissue depth and pinchability (minimum ~5–8mm for surface work), skin surface quality (stretch marks, scars, keloid history, active conditions), vascularity visibility (never pierce through visible vasculature), movement and friction zones.

When to Decline
Insufficient tissue · high vascularity at site with no safe path · active infection or lesion · keloid history in high-risk areas · anatomy that structurally prevents safe placement. Declining is professional, not personal.

Core Concept
Aseptic technique is the full collection of practices that prevent pathogenic microorganisms from reaching a wound or sterile field. You can autoclave every tool perfectly and still cause an infection if aseptic technique breaks down between sterilization and needle entering skin.

Handwashing

🖼️
[ Add Image Here ]
WHO 6-step handwashing technique poster / demonstration photo

When Handwashing Is Mandatory

  • Before entering the work area at start of shift
  • Before setting up any piercing tray
  • After touching your face, hair, phone, or clothing
  • Before donning gloves and immediately after removing gloves
  • After handling contaminated equipment or waste
  • Before and after any client contact
  • After using the restroom, eating, drinking, or any break

Proper Technique — WHO 6-Step (minimum 20 seconds)

  1. Wet hands with clean running water and apply soap
  2. Rub palms together, then back of each hand with interlaced fingers
  3. Interlace fingers palms together — rub between fingers
  4. Lock fingers, rub backs of fingers against opposite palms
  5. Rotational rub of each thumb; rub fingertips and nails against opposite palm
  6. Rinse thoroughly; dry with single-use paper towel; use towel to turn off faucet
Important
Alcohol-based hand sanitizer is NOT effective against all pathogens (C. diff, norovirus). Soap and water is always the safer default after glove removal or when hands are visibly soiled.

Selection: Nitrile only. Latex risks allergic reactions. Vinyl is not appropriate for piercing — insufficient barrier protection. Correct fit: too loose = dexterity loss and gaps; too tight = tears easily.

Mandatory Glove Changes

  • Between every client — no exceptions
  • If a glove tears at any point during the procedure
  • After touching anything outside the sterile field (phone, door handle, clothing, face)
  • After handling contaminated items before returning to a clean task

Doffing — The Step Most Piercers Rush

  1. Pinch the OUTSIDE of one glove at the wrist — do not touch your skin
  2. Peel off turning inside out — contamination contained
  3. Hold removed glove in still-gloved hand
  4. Slide two fingers of bare hand under cuff of remaining glove — inside only
  5. Peel off second glove turning inside out, enclosing first
  6. Dispose in biohazard waste immediately, then wash hands
Common Error
Snapping gloves off at the fingertips aerosolizes surface contamination. Always peel from the wrist down.

Skin Preparation

🖼️
[ Add Image Here ]
Skin prep product lineup (CHG, BZK) / concentric circle technique demo
  1. Examine the site. Active lesions, rashes, infections = postpone.
  2. Remove hair if necessary. Single-use disposable razor only. Never shared.
  3. Apply antiseptic: CHG 2–4% for most sites (not mucous membranes); BZK for nostril interior and oral sites; povidone-iodine must be fully removed before piercing.
  4. Apply outward in concentric circles. Never go back toward the center.
  5. Allow full dry time. Rushing this is a real infection risk.
  6. Mark placement with sterile skin marker or gentian violet on sterile toothpick.
  7. Do not touch prepped site with non-sterile items. If contaminated, re-prep.

Cross-Contamination Prevention

🖼️
[ Add Image Here ]
Clean vs. contaminated zone setup — overhead tray photo

Clean zone: Sterile jewelry, needles, tools. Nothing that has touched the client enters here. Contaminated zone: Used needles, removed jewelry, used gauze, used gloves. These must be physically separated — different trays or surfaces, not just "different sides."

All non-cleanable surfaces get single-use barriers before each client: arm rests, headrests, chair contact points, light handles, tray surfaces. Barriers removed from the outside and discarded after each client. Surface still gets disinfected with EPA-registered disinfectant between clients even when barriers were used.

Instant Contamination
Touching your face/mask/hair with gloved hands · Reaching across the client · Checking your phone · Picking up a dropped item · Using a product bottle for multiple clients without barrier protection on the nozzle.

Sharps Handling & Disposal

🖼️
[ Add Image Here ]
Sharps container placement in room / direct disposal demonstration
No Exceptions
Piercing needles are single-use. Needle goes from client to sharps container in one motion. Never set on any surface. Never hand to another person. Seal container at ¾ full.

Sharps containers are regulated medical waste in every state. Use dedicated medical waste services, mail-back programs, or pharmacy/health department drop-off. Never dispose in regular trash. Track all disposal for OSHA compliance.

The Non-Negotiable Rule
Anything that enters the body or contacts an open wound is either single-use pre-sterilized or it goes through the autoclave. No exceptions. No compromises.

The Hierarchy

Sanitization

Reduces microbial count to a "safe" level as defined by public health standards. Does NOT eliminate all pathogens. Appropriate for general environment cleaning only — never for anything used in a piercing procedure. This is the floor, not the ceiling.

Disinfection — Three Levels

LevelWhat It KillsUse In Studio
Low-levelMost bacteria, some viruses/fungiNon-critical surfaces: waiting room, reception desk
Intermediate-levelMost bacteria, viruses (HBV/HIV), fungi, TBClient chair, arm rests, countertops, tray surfaces after each client
High-levelAll organisms except high-concentration sporesHeat-sensitive semi-critical instruments only (rare in piercing)
Dwell Time — The Step Everyone Skips
Most piercers spray, wipe immediately, and move on. That is not disinfection — that is wiping. The disinfectant must remain wet on the surface for the full contact time on the label (30 seconds to 10 minutes). If it dries in 15 seconds, it didn't work.

Sterilization

The complete elimination of ALL forms of microbial life — including bacterial spores. The only acceptable standard for items that enter the body or contact open wounds. In piercing: needles, all jewelry, and every reusable instrument that contacts the client.

The Autoclave

An autoclave uses saturated steam under pressure at elevated temperature to achieve sterilization. Steam penetrates packaging, contacts all instrument surfaces, and denatures the proteins of every microbial cell — including the most heat-resistant bacterial endospores. There is no better, more reliable method for sterilizing metal instruments and jewelry in a studio setting.

Gravity Displacement (Class N)

Steam enters and gravity pushes air out through a drain at the bottom. Reliable for unwrapped solid instruments. Less effective for hollow items, wrapped packs, or porous loads. Most common in small studios due to lower cost. Know your unit's validated load types.

Pre-Vacuum / B-Class (Vacuum-Assisted)

Air is actively evacuated before steam enters — ensures penetration into porous materials, wrapped pouches, and hollow instruments. Superior for wrapped loads and pouched jewelry. Recommended for studios doing high volume or complex loads.

ParameterGravity CyclePre-Vac Cycle
Temperature121°C (250°F)132–135°C (270–275°F)
Pressure15 psi28–30 psi
Exposure Time15–30 minutes3–4 minutes
Dry Time15–30 minutes15–30 minutes
Wet Packs = Compromised Sterility
A wet pouch is no longer a sterile barrier. Moisture allows microorganisms to wick through packaging via capillary action. Pouches must be completely dry before they are considered sterile. Dry time is never optional.

Complete Reprocessing Workflow

Critical Point
Sterilization is not cleaning. A dirty instrument cannot be reliably sterilized. Blood and debris block steam from contacting all surfaces. Always clean before you sterilize.
  1. Transport: Used instruments into a leak-proof labeled container immediately — never carried loose
  2. Pre-soak / rinse: Remove gross contamination before scrubbing
  3. Clean: Manual scrubbing with enzymatic cleaner and a brush, or ultrasonic cleaner (preferred — cavitation removes debris mechanically)
  4. Rinse: Thorough rinsing under running water to remove all cleaner residue
  5. Inspect: Visual check for debris, damage, corrosion — damaged instruments are retired
  6. Dry completely: Moisture before autoclaving interferes with steam penetration
  7. Package: Into sterilization pouches with internal chemical indicator, labeled with contents, date, initials, and cycle number
  8. Autoclave: Correct cycle, correct parameters — don't overload, steam must circulate freely around every item
  9. Dry and inspect: Check packaging integrity after cycle. Any wet, unsealed, or damaged pouch = reprocess.
  10. Store: Dry, clean, protected storage. FIFO — first in, first out.

Indicators — How You Know It Worked

You cannot see sterilization happening. You cannot feel it or smell it. You need three types of indicators — each answering a different question about whether sterilization was actually achieved.

Mechanical Indicators — Did the machine run correctly?

The autoclave's built-in gauges: temperature, pressure, time, cycle printout. Read and record every single cycle. If the cycle didn't reach the correct temperature or ran short — that load is not sterile. Quarantine it. Do not release it.

Chemical Indicators — Did parameters reach inside the pack?

Ink or dye that changes color when exposed to sterilization parameters. Type 1 indicator tape on the outside only confirms the package went into an autoclave — it does NOT confirm sterilization. Use Type 4 or higher inside every pouch. The inside indicator tells you conditions inside the pack were actually met.

Biological Indicators (Spore Tests) — The Gold Standard

Contain live bacterial spores (Geobacillus stearothermophilus) — the most heat-resistant organisms tested. If these spores are killed, everything is killed. After the cycle, the BI is incubated at 56°C for 24–48 hours. Negative result = sterilization confirmed. Positive result = sterilization failure.

Positive Spore Test — Failure Protocol
Immediately quarantine ALL loads processed since your last confirmed negative result. Do not release any instruments. Identify and correct the failure. Run repeat tests to confirm resolution before any further reprocessing.

Spore Testing Schedule

  • Minimum: once per week, per autoclave in use — APP guideline and standard in most state regulations
  • After any autoclave maintenance, repair, or malfunction
  • After relocation of the unit or extended downtime
  • After any positive result — run multiple tests to confirm resolution
  • Know your state's specific requirements — some require testing with every load
Documentation — Mandatory
Every spore test and every cycle must be logged: date, time, cycle type, parameters reached, chemical indicator result, biological indicator result, operator initials. Undocumented testing does not exist from a legal or compliance standpoint.

Packaging & Storage

The package is not decoration — it is the sterile barrier. An instrument that exits the autoclave correctly is sterile. The moment it contacts a non-sterile surface, it is no longer sterile. Packaging maintains sterility during storage and transport from autoclave to point of use.

  • Paper/plastic peel pouches validated for steam autoclave use — not every plastic bag qualifies
  • Correct size: at least 1 inch margin on all sides. Overfilling compromises the seal.
  • Check heat seal integrity on every pouch after the cycle. Any unsealed area or puncture = repackage and re-autoclave.
  • Every pouch labeled: contents · operator initials · date of sterilization · cycle number
  • Storage: dry, clean, closed cabinets preferred. Off the floor. Away from water. FIFO.
  • Sterile packages never stored with contaminated items

Jewelry — Sterilization Specifics

Every piece of jewelry that enters a fresh piercing must be sterile at point of use. Jewelry that arrives pre-sterilized from a supplier is no longer sterile the moment the pouch is opened and handled outside a sterile field.

CAN Be Autoclaved

Implant-grade titanium (ASTM F136) · Implant-grade steel (ASTM F138 / ISO 5832-1) · Niobium · Solid 14k or 18k gold · Borosilicate glass · Implant-grade PTFE

CANNOT Be Autoclaved

Acrylic / lucite / plastic (melts or off-gases) · Sterling silver (tarnishes/corrodes in steam) · Plated jewelry (plating degrades) · Heat-sensitive gemstones (opal, turquoise, some pearls)
